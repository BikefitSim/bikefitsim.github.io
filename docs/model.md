# Bikefit Simulator calculation model

Grid: BB centre at (0, 0), X forward, Y up. Lengths in mm, angles in degrees.

Workflow: 1. Library: the frames, saddles and cockpits you own, saved once. 2. Input, with two windows side by side. Reference: as many as needed (e.g. a road and a gravel position), each either a bike fit (a frame, saddle and cockpit and how they are set up) or a personal fit (only the rider's numbers, no frame), each kind in its own tab. Target: bikes, each a frame, saddle and cockpit. Every target bike is matched to the bike fit or personal fit open in Reference. 3. Output: the changes for each target bike so the saddle tip and hand position match that reference.

Code: `src/engine.js` holds everything below; `test/engine.test.js` checks it.

## Inputs
- Frame (saved once, then picked by each bike fit and each target bike): seat tube angle, head tube angle, head tube length, BB drop, top tube (effective or actual, one is enough), seat tube (c-t or c-c, one is enough), fork length (axle to crown, plus any lower headset cup), fork offset. Stack and reach from the chart are optional overrides.
- Saved saddles (length, tip to rail centre, rail length, rail height, seatpost clamp) and cockpits (stem height; the hand position: Hand X and Hand Y from the top centre of the stem's bar clamp to where the web between thumb and index finger touches the bar; hand width, centre to centre between the hands; optionally a handlebar's drop, reach, diameter and rise, which are only drawn) are picked the same way. A reference must pick a saddle and a cockpit, and every size except the drawn bar sizes is needed: an empty one is listed as missing (no built-in defaults since 2026-11-12).
- Bike fit, besides its frame, saddle and cockpit: saddle height (BB centre to saddle top along the seat tube), saddle setback (horizontal, BB centre to a plumb line from the saddle tip), spacers under the stem (head tube top to stem, top cap included), stem length and angle.
- Personal fit, besides its saddle and cockpit: saddle height as the straight line from the BB centre to the saddle tip, saddle setback, personal reach (straight tape line, saddle tip to the hand point) and hand drop (vertical, saddle top to the hand point). Target bikes keep its cockpit's Hand X and Hand Y.
- Each target bike: a frame, and optionally a saddle and a cockpit; none picked = the reference's. It keeps the reference's hand position, unless it picks a saved cockpit, which brings its own stem height and hand position.
- Fixed assumptions: a stem's bar clamp is as tall as the stem (40 mm when no stem height is given), so its top centre sits half the stem height above the bar centre, and its steerer clamp centre half the stem height above the spacers. The top tube meets the head tube 18 mm below its top. With only one seat tube length, c-t = c-c + 20 mm; results that rest on that are flagged.

## Frame
- Stack = BB drop + (fork length + head tube length) × sin(HTA) − fork offset × cos(HTA)
- Reach = effective top tube − stack ÷ tan(STA)
- Without an effective top tube, reach comes from the actual one, which runs centre to centre from P = c-c × (−cos STA, sin STA) on the seat tube to J = T − 18 × d on the head tube: reach = P.x + √(actual² − (stack − 18 × sin HTA − P.y)²) − 18 × cos HTA. With both top tubes, the effective one is used and reaches more than 8 mm apart are flagged.
- Seatpost showing = (S.y − rail height) ÷ sin(STA) − seat tube c-t, checked against 40 and 280 mm.
- Head tube top T = (reach, stack). Steering axis direction d = (−cos HTA, sin HTA).
- Calculated stack and reach rarely match a chart exactly: a lower headset cup missing from fork length (10 mm of cup ≈ 9.5 mm of stack), rounded angles (0.5° of seat angle ≈ 5.5 mm of reach) and brand-specific definitions of seat angle and top tube. Chart values, when entered, are used directly.

## Fit points (per reference)
- Hand position H: the hand point. Top centre of the bar clamp C = H − (Hand X, Hand Y), measured level and vertically; bar centre B = C − (0, stem height ÷ 2).
- Bike fit: saddle tip S = (−setback, saddle height × sin(STA_ref)). Stem clamp Q = T_ref + (spacers + stem height ÷ 2) × d; bar centre B = Q + length × (cos θ, sin θ), θ = 90° − HTA + stem angle; H = B + (Hand X, Hand Y + stem height ÷ 2). Then drop = S.y − H.y, side-view reach h = H.x − S.x and personal reach = √(h² + drop² + (hand width ÷ 2)²). These are used for target bikes exactly as if they had been measured. "Copy as personal fit" stores them with the straight line |S| as saddle height.
- Personal fit: S = (−setback, √(saddle line² − setback²)), where the saddle line is the straight tape from the BB centre to the saddle tip. It reads about 30 mm less than a saddle height along the seat tube, because the tip sits ahead of the seat tube. The tape from the saddle tip (on the centreline) to the hand runs out to the side, so h = √(personal reach² − drop² − (hand width ÷ 2)²), and H = (S.x + h, S.y − drop). Ignoring a 400 mm hand width overstates h by about 31 mm on a 654 mm tape, roughly a whole stem size.
- Because personal reach is measured from the saddle tip, more setback moves H back by the same amount, so the stem gets shorter (about 1 mm per 1 mm of setback).

## Output for each target bike
- Personal reach (the tape length) and drop are kept. With the same hand width the hands stay at H. With another hand width (a target that picks a saved cockpit), the hands move level to H_t, where the tape from the saddle tip still reads the same personal reach: H_t.x = S.x + √(personal reach² − drop² − (hand width ÷ 2)²). The target's stem has to put the top of its bar clamp at H_t − (Hand X, Hand Y).
- You sit in the same place on the back of any saddle, so with a saddle of another length the back of the saddle stays put and the tip moves: a saddle 30 mm shorter has its tip 30 mm further back (30 mm more setback). With the same saddle length the saddle tip S never moves; what changes is how it is read on a different frame. Saddle top height and the hands stay put either way.
- Saddle height along its seat tube = S.y ÷ sin(STA); a steeper seat tube reads less for the same point (e.g. 735 → 729.6 mm for about 1.4° steeper).
- Setback from BB: unchanged.
- Saddle on rails: the saddle tip's distance ahead of the seatpost clamp, less tip to rail centre, is where the saddle sits on its rails (+ forward of their middle). The app tries an inline and a 20 mm offset seatpost (an offset moves the clamp back by offset ÷ sin STA) and uses the one nearer the middle; the clamp has to stay within the rail markings (up to 5 mm past is a warning, more an error, on the reference bike too). A target's rail move is its rail position less the reference's, shown when the reference's saddle is on its rails. A personal fit has no seatpost, so only the tip's distance ahead of the seat tube line at saddle height is given.
- Seatpost clamp height: the clamp sits `railH` (rail height: saddle top to rails, vertical, at the clamp; per saddle) below the saddle top, so it is railH ÷ tan STA further forward on the seat tube line than the saddle top. Each extra mm of rail height lets the saddle tip sit 1 ÷ tan STA mm (about 0.3 mm) less far ahead of the clamp.
- Stem and spacers: bar centre target B = H_t − (Hand X, Hand Y + stem height ÷ 2), with the reference's Hand X and Y (or the target's saved cockpit's) and the target's stem height. Bar centre for a stem = T + (spacers + stem height ÷ 2) × d + length × (cos θ, sin θ), θ = 90° − HTA + stem angle. Every stem length and angle on hand is tried, spacers rounded to the spacer step within 0..max.
- Match: a target bike matches the hand position when the closest stem and spacers miss it by 10 mm or less (`HAND_TOL`, straight-line distance); beyond that Output shows a problem line, but still shows the closest options, the results and the drawings.
- Ranking: by miss distance as shown (0.1 mm), so the first option is always the closest; ties go to the stem angle nearest the reference's (−6° for a personal fit, which has no stem), then to fewer spacers. The exact (unrounded) length and spacers for the chosen angle are shown too.
- With a personal fit there is no reference bike, so results are the full setup, without "vs reference" differences.

## Limitations
- The handlebar is only representative: the calculation only uses the hand position, so Hand X and Hand Y have to be measured for the position you ride (tops, hoods, drops, flat bar grips). It is drawn with level drops and a 175° bend (the tops fall 5° from the clamp to where the bend starts, 30 mm behind the reach); hoods are not drawn.
- A saddle of another shape can still change where you sit, even with the back of the saddle kept in place; inputs are measured at the saddle tip, so a personal reach taped on one saddle reads differently on a saddle of another length (Output warns when the lengths differ).
- The simulation isn't possible when the same saddle position can't be reached: an error when neither an inline nor a 20 mm offset seatpost keeps the clamp within 5 mm of the rail markings.

## Drawings
- Side view of the target bike (blue, solid) over the reference bike (yellow, dashed), aligned at the BB on a 50 mm grid; saddle tips as rings and hands as diamonds in each bike's colour, with a red diamond for the expected hand position when the target's cockpit differs and no stem and spacers come within 10 mm of it. A personal fit has no bike to draw; its BB to saddle tip line is drawn in yellow instead. A flat bar (reach 0) is drawn end-on. Dimensions: saddle height along the seat tube, setback, hand X/Y, drop, personal reach. The top tube is drawn to the seat tube c-c point when one is given, otherwise to the top of the seat tube. Close-ups of the front end and saddle on 10 mm grids. Layers for reference, dimensions and grid.

## Saving
- Saved shape stays v3; new fields are only added with defaults, so older saves keep loading. References are a list (`refs`, each with `kind` 'bike' (bike fit) or 'fit' (personal fit) and its own `fit`; a personal fit's saddle line is `fit.saddleLine`; `lastRef` remembers the one last open in each tab, and a missing kind gets an empty one on load). A reference without `kind` is a bike fit; default names from earlier versions ("Bike reference 2", "Fit reference 1", "Bike fit 3") are renamed to "Reference bike 2", "Personal fit 1", "Reference bike 3"; if it gave the hands by personal reach and drop, the stem those imply is filled in (0.1 mm, 0.1°) and flagged until edited. A save from before that held a single `ref` + `fit` loads as the first reference. Target bikes carry a `refId`, only used when old saves are converted (see Library below). A target bike without `barWidth` uses the reference width.
- `fit.barWidth` missing in older saves is loaded as 0 (personal reach read in side view), so their results don't shift; the page asks for the width.
- Start: one empty bike fit ("Reference bike 1"), one empty personal fit ("Personal fit 1") and one empty target bike ("Target bike 1"). Bikes are kept in the browser.
- Copies: browser and an optional data file on the computer (`{app: 'bikefitsim', format: 3, savedAt, state}`); each carries the time of the last edit and the newest wins. A share link carries the whole state after `#s=`: 'c' + base64url of deflate-raw JSON (or 'p' + plain JSON where the browser can't compress) of a positional array `[1, refs, bikes, saddles, bars, defaults, opts (0 in older links: the old starting stem lengths 60 to 140, angles ±6/10/17, 5 mm step, 40 mm max), selected ref index, selected bike index, frames, 1]` (the final 1 marks a link made since saddle and cockpit sizes became required), with fields by position from the LINK_* key lists in app.js (only ever append to them), saved frames, saddles and cockpits as 1-based list positions (a bike that picks a frame sends an empty frame-size row and the frame's position at the end of its row) and default names as their number; a fresh page opens it directly, otherwise it asks to replace, and replacing stops writing to a linked data file so the file keeps its bikes.
- Library (step 1): `frames` [{id, name, sta, hta, htLength, ett, att, seatTube, seatTubeCC, bbDrop, forkA2C, forkOffset, stack, reach, chainstay, wheelbase, tyre, wheel}], `saddles` [{id, name, saddleLen, tipToRails, railTravel, railH, clampLen}] and `bars` (cockpits) [{id, name, stemH, handX, handY, barWidth (hand width), hbDrop, hbReach, hbDiam, hbRise}] (the hand position is used, the bar sizes only drawn), with `selPart` the one open in each list. A bike fit or target bike picks its frame with `frameId`; references (in `fit`) and target bikes pick a saddle and cockpit with `saddleId` / `barId`. A picked frame's sizes are copied onto every bike that picks it (`syncFrames`), so the rest of the code reads them there. An empty frame pick, or an empty saddle or cockpit pick on a reference, is listed as missing; on a target bike an empty saddle or cockpit pick is the reference's. A frame's empty tyre and wheel still take 30 mm and 700c (only the ground view and drawings use them). Deleting a saved one leaves whatever picked it with no pick.
- Older saves and links kept frame, saddle and cockpit sizes on each bike (until 2026-11-12). On load (`toLibrary`) each set of sizes becomes a saved frame (named after its bike), saddle or cockpit ("Saddle N", "Cockpit N"), reusing a saved one with the same sizes, and the bike picks it; a target bike's empty sizes, which meant its reference's, are filled from the reference open at the time, so results stay the same. Saves from before the hand position held `barReach` and `barWidth`, which load as the bar's reach and the hand width, while a reference's own or picked reach r becomes Hand X = max(0, r − 30), Hand Y = −4. There is no Default references section any more (removed 2026-11-11): a save or link that set those values gets them written into the empty sizes it used, and a saved saddle without a clamp takes the clamp of the first reference or target bike that picked it. Saves and links without `sizes: 1` (link: no final 1) used built-in sizes for empty saddle and cockpit sizes and for a reference with none picked; on load (`fillBuiltins`) those sizes (saddle 265 long, 125 tip to rail centre, 60 rails, 40 rail height, 35 clamp; cockpit 40 stem height, Hand X 50, Hand Y −4, 380 hand width) are written into the saved parts, and such references pick a saved "Default saddle" / "Default cockpit", so results stay the same.
