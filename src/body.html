Summary

Seat tube angle

From the geometry chart, or measured from horizontal with the bike level.

Head tube angle

From the geometry chart, or measured from horizontal with the bike level.

Head tube length

End to end along the head tube.

BB drop

How far the BB centre sits below a line between the two axles.

Top tube (eff.)

Measured level from the top of the head tube back to the seat tube line. Enter this, the actual top tube or the reach.

Top tube (actual)

Along the top tube, centre to centre. Enter this, the effective top tube or the reach.

Seat tube (c-t)

BB centre to the top of the seat tube. Enter this or the c-c.

Seat tube (c-c)

BB centre to the centre of the top tube junction. Enter this or the c-t.

Fork length

Axle to crown race, plus any lower headset cup. Optional when you enter the stack.

Fork offset

Also called rake: how far the front axle sits ahead of the steering axis.

Stack

Height of the top of the head tube above the BB centre. When given, it is used instead of the one worked out from the tubes.

Reach

Level distance from the BB centre to the top of the head tube. When given, it is used instead of the one worked out from the tubes.

Chainstay

BB centre to rear axle. Places the rear wheel. With it on both frames, they are also drawn on level ground.

Wheelbase

Axle to axle. With the chainstay, places the front wheel exactly.

Tyre width

Tyre width. Only used in the drawings: wheel size, BB height and trail.

Wheel size

The rim size. Like the tyre width, only used in the drawings. A target bike follows its reference until you pick another size.

Saddle height on a bikefit

BB centre to the top of the saddle, along the seat tube.

Saddle height on a personal fit

Straight line from the BB centre to the saddle tip. It reads about 30 mm less than along the seat tube.

Saddle setback

With the bike level, hang a plumb line from the saddle tip and measure level from it back to the BB centre.

Frame

Pick a frame saved in Library, where its geometry is entered.

Saddle

Pick a saddle saved in Library. A reference needs one; on a target bike, Same as reference uses the reference's saddle.

Saddle length

Measured level from the tip to the back of the saddle.

Tip to rail centre

Measured level from the saddle tip to a point above the middle of the rail markings.

Saddle rail length

Length of the adjustment markings on the rails. The seatpost clamp has to stay inside them.

Rail height

Vertical distance from the top of the saddle down to the rails, at the seatpost clamp.

Seatpost clamp

Length of the seatpost's rail clamp, front to back. Saved with the saddle, since it decides how far the saddle can slide.

Spacers under stem

Everything between the top of the head tube and the stem, top cap included.

Stem length

As printed on the stem: steerer centre to bar clamp centre.

Stem angle

As printed on the stem. Negative points down, for example −6.

Personal reach

Straight line from the saddle tip to your hand point, where the web between thumb and index finger touches the bar.

Hand drop

Vertical distance from the saddle top down to your hand point. Negative if the hands are higher.

Stem height

Height of the stem where it clamps the steerer.

Hand X

Measured level from the top centre of the stem's bar clamp to your hand point. Forward is positive. Tops, hoods, drops or flat bar grips all work.

Hand Y

Measured vertically from the top centre of the stem's bar clamp to your hand point. Up is positive.

Hand width

Centre to centre between your hands at that position.

Cockpit

Pick a cockpit saved in Library. A reference needs one; on a target bike, Same as reference keeps the reference's stem height and hand position. A target bike that picks one uses its stem height, hand position and hand width.

Bar drop

Centre of the tops to centre of the drops. Only drawn.

Bar reach

From the bar centre forward to the front of the bend. Only drawn.

Bar diameter

Where the stem clamps it. Empty is 31.8. Only drawn.

Bar rise

How far the tops rise above the bar centre at the stem clamp. Empty is 0. Only drawn: Hand Y already includes it.

Stem lengths

Stem lengths you can use, separated by commas.

Stem angles

Stem angles you can use, separated by commas. Leave out the positive ones if you won't flip a stem.

Spacer step

Your thinnest spacer. The options go up in steps of this.

Min spacers

The fewest spacers you would run. Empty is 0.

Max spacers

The most spacers you would run.

Known limitations

  • Simulation isn't possible if the same saddle position is not achievable.
  • Manufacturers' geometry charts aren't always exact.
  • Every saddle is taken to be level.
  • Crank length affects a bikefit, every bike is assumed to use the same.
  • Results are estimates. Check the setup on the bike before riding.
