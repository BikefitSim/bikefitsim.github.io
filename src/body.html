How to measure

Saddle height

On a bike reference: from the BB centre to the top of the saddle, following the seat tube line.

Saddle height on a fit reference

A straight tape from the BB centre to the saddle tip. With the setback it places the tip without knowing the seat angle. It reads less than along the seat tube, about 30 mm on a typical road fit.

Saddle setback

Hang a plumb line (a string with a small weight) from the saddle tip so it hangs straight down. Setback is the horizontal distance from the string back to the BB centre. Keep the bike level.

Personal reach and bar width

A straight tape from the saddle tip to the base of the hoods, where the hood meets the bar. On a bar without hoods, tape to where your hands sit. That line runs out to the side, so the calculator takes away half the bar width (and the drop) to get the side-view distance. Bar width is centre to centre at the hoods.

Stem on a bike reference

A bike reference takes the stem fitted on it instead of personal reach and drop. With the frame, spacers and handlebar reach, that places your hands, and the panel shows the personal reach and drop it works out to. "Copy as fit reference" keeps those numbers without the bike.

Handlebar reach

From the bar's clamp centre forward to the base of the hoods, from the bar's spec. 0 means a flat bar: hands right over the bar.

Handlebar drop

Vertical, from the top of the saddle down to the top of the handlebar. Use a level from the saddle. Negative if the bar is higher than the saddle.

Spacers

Everything between the top of the head tube and the bottom of the stem, headset top cap included. On target bikes the result is measured the same way.

Stack from the tubes

Stack = BB drop + (fork length + head tube length) × sin(head angle) − fork offset × cos(head angle). Reach = top tube − stack ÷ tan(seat angle). Chart stack and reach, when filled in, are used instead.

Limitations

  • The hand position is the base of the hoods, level with the top of the bar. Personal reach has to be measured to that point, or to where your hands sit on a bar without hoods. Hands on the drops or the tops are not modelled.
  • Handlebar reach 0 is read as a flat bar, with the hands over the bar centre.
  • Handlebar rise and drop are not calculated yet. The base of the hoods is taken as level with the top of the bar at the clamp, so a riser bar, or a bar whose hoods sit higher or lower than that, puts the hands off by that much.
All lengths in mm, angles in degrees. Grid: X forward of the BB centre, Y above it. Assumes a 40 mm tall stem and a 31.8 mm bar.
